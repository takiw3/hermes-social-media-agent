# Media rights and disclosure

This document describes how the profile behaves. It is not legal advice. The profile flags an issue, cites the official source, and escalates.

## Asset rights

Every project has an `asset-rights-register.yaml` listing each font, sound, image, video, and registry block with its source, license, proof, and restrictions.

- An asset with no recorded license is unusable.
- The launcher refuses a final render while any asset in the project folder lacks a complete entry.
- A platform's music library licenses use inside that platform. The profile does not assume a sound is cleared for a rendered file posted elsewhere, for a business account, or for a sponsored post.
- The agent does not fetch a video, sound, or image from a social platform to reuse it. Deny rules block the common download tools.

## Disclosure

Sponsored, gifted, affiliate, and paid-partnership content needs disclosure. The agent blocks a piece until the disclosure is in.

United States baseline, from FTC guidance read on 2026-10-06:

- Disclose any financial, employment, personal, or family relationship with a brand, and anything of value received to mention a product, including free or discounted products.
- Put the disclosure where it is hard to miss, with the message. Not only on a profile page, not at the end, not behind a "more" link, not mixed into a group of hashtags.
- For video, the disclosure belongs in the video itself and not only in the description, ideally spoken and shown.
- Use plain words such as "ad", "advertisement", or "sponsored", or a plain statement such as thanking a brand for a free product. Terms such as "sp", "spon", "collab", or a standalone "thanks" or "ambassador" are not enough.
- A platform's disclosure tool is used in addition to your own disclosure, not instead of it.
- Owners and employees endorsing their own company's products should say so in the post. An employer listed in a profile is not sufficient.

Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers and https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking. The FTC site refused the headless browser, so these pages were read through a fetch-and-summarize tool; the wording above follows that summary.

For another jurisdiction, the agent asks which authority you follow. It does not guess local rules.

## Platform disclosure settings

Read from official pages on 2026-10-06.

| Platform | What the platform requires | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | Select the paid promotion setting for branded content, sponsorships, or endorsements. It adds a label at the start of the video. | https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235 |
| Instagram, Facebook | Tag the business partner so the "Paid partnership with" label shows. Branded content includes gifted products. Content with an affiliate link should carry the label. | https://www.facebook.com/business/help/788160621327601 |
| TikTok | Turn on the content disclosure setting for any content promoting a brand, product, or service, including your own business. Your own business is labeled "Promotional content"; a third party's is labeled "Paid partnership". | https://www.tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=7636670088170904085 |
| LinkedIn | Turn on the Brand partnership toggle for content shared in exchange for value. Public posts only. | https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1627083 |
| X | Not verified. X's help pages refused every automated read. | https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/paid-partnerships-policy |

The TikTok rule is the one owners miss: promoting your own business also needs the setting on.

## AI-generated content

| Platform | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | Disclose AI content that makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not, alters footage of a real event or place, generates a realistic scene that did not occur, or creates music that is the main focus. Non-realistic content and minor edits, including caption creation and script or thumbnail help, do not need disclosure. | https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 |
| TikTok | Label AI content containing realistic images, audio, or video. TikTok may auto-label, including from C2PA Content Credentials. Some AI content is not allowed even if labeled. | https://www.tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=7636670084747893268 |
| Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, X | Not verified for this release. | see the facts file |

Captions, lower-thirds, and motion graphics made with HyperFrames are ordinary edits under the YouTube examples above. If you add anything AI-generated that looks real, the agent applies the platform rule or asks you.

## Claims

Health, financial, legal, earnings, and before-and-after claims are blocked until you or your Executive profile confirm they are substantiated and permitted.

## People

Recorded consent is required for any identifiable person on camera, any client named, and any minor. Content involving a minor always escalates. A voice clone, avatar, or likeness of a real person is refused without that person's recorded consent, and voice and avatar generation are off in this release in any case.

## Competitors

No competitor logo, footage, or name is used in a way that implies endorsement.
