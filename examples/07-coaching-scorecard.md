# Example: coaching feedback on a draft

Synthetic example. The business, the people, the files, and every figure are invented to show the shape of the output. Nothing here is a real result, a benchmark, or a recommendation.

**Owner asks:** "Review this draft and give me the top three fixes."

| Dimension | Score | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 2 | Eleven words of greeting before the idea arrives |
| Clarity | 4 | One idea, stated plainly once it starts |
| Pacing | 3 | The middle beat runs long with no visual change |
| Proof | 4 | A real job sheet is on screen |
| Payoff | 4 | The habit is concrete and doable |
| Call to action | 3 | Two asks at the end. Pick one. |
| Delivery | 3 | Eyes drop to notes on each new sentence |
| Packaging | 2 | Cover text repeats the caption |

**Top fix:** start on the line you reach at second seven. "Your busiest month can lose you money." Everything before it goes.

**Fixes**

| Change | Why it matters | How to do it next time | Evidence or taste |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open on the point | The first two seconds decide whether the rest is heard | Write the first line last, then film it first | Taste, with a test designed to check it |
| One call to action | Two asks split the response | Decide the ask before you write | Taste |
| Cover the client name on the job sheet | A named client needs consent | Keep a cover card in the desk drawer | Rule, not opinion |

**What worked:** you showed the actual sheet. Keep doing that.

**Practice goal this week:** three takes where your first word is the point.
